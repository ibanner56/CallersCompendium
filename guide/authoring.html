Write & edit dances

Sooner or later you will want to put a dance in yourself — a dance you wrote, one a friend taught you at a festival, or one that came in from an import with a figure the app could not quite read. This guide covers the dance editor: how figures go in, where the notes and credits live, and how the app keeps your work safe while you type.

Finding your way around these words. On-screen buttons and screens are written in bold — like New dance, Save, and Figures. The first time a dance term appears it links to App terms, so you can get a plain-language definition without losing your place.

If you have not entered a dance before, the Getting started guide walks through one end to end. This guide is the fuller reference.

Open the editor

There are three ways in:

  • New dance, from your collection — starts a blank dance. The screen is titled New dance.
  • Edit, from a dance's detail view — opens that dance. The screen is titled Edit dance.
  • Import and edit, on a row in the import review list — brings that one dance in straight away and opens it, so you can fix a stubborn figure while the rest of the import waits for you. It is not covered by the import's Undo.

The editor is a full screen with Save at the end of the top bar, after undo and redo; on a phone it shows as a check mark. The title is the only required field; everything else can stay empty until you know it.

Enter the figures

Figures is the heart of the editor. Each figure is one row, in the order the dance is called, and the app tracks how many beats they add up to as you go.

Type and press Enter

Figure entry is keyboard-first. Choose Add figure (or Add first figure on an empty dance). When the dance can also hold a meanwhile, a modifier or a sequence, the button opens a small menu: press Space or Enter on it and focus lands on Add figure. Press Enter to choose it, use the up and down arrow keys to reach the other choices, and press Escape to close the menu.

With Free-text entry off, Add figure adds a row with its Move box focused. As you type there, the app offers matching moves; press Enter to accept one and it is added with that move's default settings. Typing sw and pressing Enter, for example, gives you a swing.

The moves offered are those of the dance's Type: an English dance offers English figures, such as set, turn single, siding and the heys, and a contra dance offers contra figures, and a square dance offers square calls, such as promenade, allemande left, circle, chain and separate. A figure the dance already holds from another type stays as it is; it just isn't offered for a new row. Square calls are picked here for now: a line you type with Free-text entry in a square dance is kept as you typed it, as a custom line, because the app can't read square calls from text yet. It never turns a square's line into a contra figure.

With Free-text entry on, Add figure opens Type a figure instead: one box that takes a whole line and offers no move suggestions. Press Enter and the line is added; sw typed there on its own is an unrecognized figure, not a swing. See Free-text entry below.

Anything you add that the app cannot recognize is still kept — it becomes a unrecognized figure, holding your text. Nothing you add is ever thrown away because the app did not understand it; the only change is that role words are saved as role terms and shown in your own dialect (see below).

The dance editor while entering figures, with recognized rows and the running beat total visible below the list.

The box stays open after each figure so you can keep going: type, Enter, type, Enter. When you have entered all your figures, choose Finish adding: it adds a line still in the box, as Enter would, and then closes the box. Press Enter on a blank box to close it too. Escape closes the box without adding what is in it.

A line you have typed but not added is not lost if you move on. Save adds it first, as Enter would, and leaving the editor with a line in the box asks Discard changes?.

Free-text entry

Turn on Free-text entry in Settings ▸ Dialect ▸ Dance details & shorthands and the same box accepts a whole line at a time instead of one move at a time. Type neighbor balance & swing and you get one figure, a swing with a balance; type 16 circle left 3/4 and you get a sixteen-beat circle left three-quarters. A line that states no count starts with the figure's usual beats, and they follow the figure if you change its options. Your Move defaults apply as they do when you pick the move, but only to what the line leaves out: with a Move default of a full hey, hey gives a full hey and half hey still a half hey. In an English country dance, the line is read as English figures (1st corners set and turn single, Partner R shoulder round, circle left half way), never as contra moves; anything it can't read exactly is kept as you typed it. It reads callers' shorthand too: P and N are partner and neighbor, trade is change places (First corners trade by right, or trade by right shoulder: either way the dancers pass by the right), Pass P by L passes partner by the left shoulder, All Partner Set and Ones Partner Set are partners and the ones setting, Partner Set R & L sets right first, and 1s cross and go below is a cross and a cast down one place. A detail a figure has no option for is kept as its note: turn single right forward is a turn single with the note "forward", and Second corners cast partner follow a cast with the note "partner follow". A circle or star "half way" counts places from the dance's formation, two in a duple minor and three in a triple minor. A line that starts with a bar range (1-4 partners set and turn single, (5-8) circle left, A1 1-8 lead up) counts those bars by the dance's Meter: 1-4 is 8 beats in 2/2 and 12 in 3/2 or 3/4. With no meter, a bar counts the beats of the phrase structure, as the Bars field does. Where the phrase structure's phrases differ in beats per bar (A:2*4*3 + B:1*8*2, an A in 3/4 and a B in 6/8), the Meter does not decide it: each bar is as long as the phrase it falls in, and the bars count from where the line is placed in the dance, so the numbers you type give how many bars, not where they sit. In that example 1-4 placed first is 12 beats, and 9-12 placed after the two A phrases is 8. A line that runs across the change counts each bar by its own phrase. The bars are the count. A line that also states a count in beats (1-4 (6) circle left) is kept as you typed it, with the bars' count, so you can see the two disagree and fix whichever is wrong. A line with several bar ranges ((5-8) circle left (9-12) circle right) is kept as you typed it too, counted across all of them (or by its first range, past 64 beats); type each figure on its own line to have them read. A line under the box tells you how many figures it added and invites you to type another (a screen reader announces the count too); it goes away when you start typing.

The same works on a figure that is already in the dance. Open it, type a line into its move over what is there, and press Enter: if the line is not simply the name of a move, it is read the same way and the figure is replaced by what you typed, in its place. A line that reads as several figures replaces the one figure with all of them. The figure's note, its walkthrough step and its progression mark are kept, as they are when you pick a new move: the note and the step go on the first of the new figures (after any note you typed with the line) and the progression mark on the last. Typing a move's name and choosing it still works as before. With Free-text entry off, a line typed there is kept as an unrecognized figure holding your text.

Pressing Enter in a figure's Move box chooses, in this order: the move (or your own figure) whose name is exactly what you typed, so star is a star and not a star through; then, with Free-text entry on, your shorthand with that token, so a short token such as rl is not taken for the first move whose name contains it; and only then the first suggestion offered.

If you leave a figure's Move box without pressing Enter, by moving to another box, saving the dance (or a combination or shorthand you are editing), or closing the figure or opening another one, what you typed is used only when it is exactly a move's name (or one of your own figures' names) or, with Free-text entry on, one of your shorthands. Anything else, such as part of a name, goes back to the move the figure had: leaving the box never picks a suggestion for you or makes an unrecognized figure. On a phone the same goes for closing the list of moves without choosing one. Going back with such a name or shorthand still in the box asks before discarding it.

With Free-text entry on, a new dance starts with no figures, so there are no placeholders to delete first, unless you have saved your own starting figures.

It also understands common phrasings such as N swing, neighbors balance and swing, half hey, ladies start by the right, 1/4 hey, partner start right shoulder and allemande left 1 1/2 with neighbor. When the app reads the start of a line as a figure, the rest of the line becomes that figure's note (neighbors swing slowly is a swing with the note "slowly"). That happens only when the rest cannot change what the figure is. If the rest is and back, a bare number (neighbors swing 16), a turn amount, in figures or in words (petronella twice, petronella two times, circle left 3/4 and pass through, allemande left three quarters), or another move after a comma, and or then (partners balance and swing, end facing down), the whole line is kept as an unrecognized figure, marked unparsed. Otherwise you would get a figure with the wrong beats or turn, with the rest hidden in its note. Use the leading 16 neighbors swing form to set the beats.

Dancers can be named the way the figure list names them: prev neighbor swing, third partner allemande left once, second shadow balance, same roles dosido once, centers two hand turn once, or, in your dialect's role words, first lark two hand turn once and next larks swing (the larks among your next neighbors).

It also reads figures the way Caller's Companion writes them, each as one figure: Circle L, Neighbor alle R 1.5 and R&L (a right and left through); New Neighbor swing (your next neighbors) and Corner allemande left (your first corners); Ring balance, spin to the right (a petronella with its balance); Balance wave and slide to the right (a Rory O'More with its balance); Wave balance, circulate (Larks cross) (a box circulate with its balance, the Larks crossing); Partner R-hand balance, square through 2; and make short waves.

Any move's own name typed on its own works, whatever your dialect: hey, mad robin, contra corners, give & take (or give and take, give-and-take), in capitals or not, with or without a full stop, comma or exclamation mark after it (hey.). Your dialect's own name for a move works too. A name on its own gives the figure with its usual options, which you can then change. Put ones, twos, threes, fours, partners, neighbors, everyone, role1s, role2s (or the singular partner or neighbor), or any other dancers the way the figure list names them (same roles, third corners, prev neighbor), in front of the name of a move that takes dancers and they are the dancers: neighbor pass by, ones arch and dive, third corners gate. A move that does not take dancers that way (hey, slice), a word the app does not know in the dancer's place, or anything else on the line stays an unrecognized figure unless the line is read as above. The app also reads hey for four, half hey, full hey, turn as a couple, turn amounts in words (allemande right once and a half), and older role words such as ravens by what they mean (ravens chain is the same as robins chain), unless your own role terms use that word, in which case it means your role. hey for four (or hey for 4) is a full hey, 16 beats, unless you give it a count of 8, as in (8) hey for four, which makes it a half hey. hey for 3 is the hey for three, as hey for three is.

A count can also be written in brackets, (16) circle left or [16] circle left, and goes at the end as well: circle left (16) or circle left [16]. Any of these can say beats: neighbor swing (16 beats), neighbor swing 16 beats or 16 beats neighbor swing. A count in bars (circle left 4 bars, circle left (4 bars), 4 bars circle left) is kept as a note on the figure ("4 bars") and the rest of the line is read as usual, because how many beats a bar holds depends on the dance's music. The figure keeps the beats it has when you pick it from the list. If the rest of the line is not read as a figure, the whole line is kept as an unrecognized figure, as you typed it. A count is never dropped: if the line states one the app cannot use (a second count on the same figure), the line is kept whole as an unrecognized figure. A line kept as an unrecognized figure keeps your words as you typed them, count included, and the count you gave is also its beats.

A count too long to be beats, five digits or more (circle left 10000 beats, (10000) circle left), is not used as one. The figure is read as usual and takes the beats it has when you pick it from the list, and the number is kept in the figure's note (10000 beats) so you can see it was typed. If the rest of the line is not read as a figure, the whole line is kept as an unrecognized figure, as you typed it. A count that is shorter but more than the 64 beats a figure can take (circle left (100)) keeps the whole line as an unrecognized figure too.

Pasting several lines into the box keeps them as one line, with a space where each line break was. To add several lines at once, one figure line each, use Add several figures at once.

A ; between figures makes a list, and each part is read as its own figure: circle left 3/4; turn alone gives two figures, in order. A count stays with the part it follows: circle left (8); turn alone gives the circle 8 beats, and circle left; turn alone (4) gives the turn 4. If any part cannot be read as a figure, or a count cannot be matched to a figure (a part that became a note on the one before it), the whole line is kept as one unrecognized figure instead. A || makes its two sides a meanwhile: circle left || neighbor swing gives one meanwhile group whose sides are the circle and the swing.

As always, anything it cannot parse is kept as an unrecognized figure rather than dropped.

Add several figures at once

To bring in a dance you have as text, from an email, a web page or a typed document, open Add figure ▸ Add figures from text… under the figure list. Type or paste the figures into the box, one figure per line:

A1 (8) Neighbor balance & swing
   (8) Long lines forward and back
A2 (8) Ladies chain

Each line is read as the Move box reads a line you type with Free-text entry on, with your shorthands and your dialect, whether Free-text entry is on or not. Blank lines are skipped, and so is a line that is only a phrase label, such as an A1 heading. In a contra dance a phrase label such as A1 or B2 at the start of a line is left out, so A1 (8) Ladies chain reads as (8) Ladies chain. English and square dances keep it: an English line such as A1 1-8 lead up is read with its label, because the label comes before a bar range; other English lines that start with a label may not be recognized, so leave the label off them.

As you type, the sheet shows how many figures each line gives and marks the lines it does not recognize. Those are still added, each as an unrecognized figure that keeps its text, for you to fix. Add figures adds them all at the end of the figure list as one step, so a single Undo takes them out again. Cancel adds nothing. Nothing is saved until you save the dance.

The sheet reads up to 200 lines and 20,000 characters at a time, and up to 2,000 characters on one line. Longer text is refused, and nothing from it is added: add it in parts.

Read the figures from a picture

On iPhone, iPad, Mac and Windows, From an image… in the same sheet reads the text of a picture you choose, such as a photo of an index card or a page of a book, and puts it in the box in place of what is there. Check it and correct it there: the lines are then read exactly as typed text is.

  • The picture is read on your device, by the text recognition built into iOS, macOS and Windows. Nothing is sent anywhere, and nothing is downloaded to read it.
  • The picture is not kept. It is read once and let go; only the text reaches the sheet. Nothing about the picture is saved with the dance, synced or backed up. A copy the picker makes for the app is deleted once read.
  • It reads the app's language and English. Where the device can't read the app's language (some languages, such as Japanese, need a newer iOS or macOS, and some aren't offered at all), the sheet says so and reads English. Windows reads one language at a time: the app's language when its language pack is installed in Windows Settings, otherwise English. The app never installs one. If neither is installed, From an image… stays off and the sheet says why.
  • On Windows, HEIC photos (the format iPhones save in) can be read only once the HEIF and HEVC extensions from the Microsoft Store are installed; otherwise the sheet says the device can't open it. A JPEG or PNG always works.
  • A picture over 25 MB, or over 10,000 pixels on a side or 50 million pixels in all, is refused before anything is read. Crop it or choose a smaller one.
  • Typed text reads best. Handwriting may come out wrong, so check each line.

On iPhone and iPad, Take a photo beside it opens the camera instead, so you can photograph the card without leaving the app. The first time, the system asks whether the app may use the camera; it uses it only when you tap Take a photo. The photo is read the same way and is not kept: it is deleted as soon as its text has been read, or straight away if reading fails, and it is never added to your photo library. If you said no to the camera, you can turn it on again in the Settings app, under Caller's Compendium.

From an image… isn't offered on Android or Linux, and Take a photo is only on iPhone and iPad: you can still type or paste the figures.

Figure shorthands

If you type the same run of figures over and over, teach it to the app once as a shorthand. Choose Figure shorthands in Settings ▸ Dialect ▸ Dance details & shorthands, then New shorthand:

  • Shorthand is the exact line (or the exact part of a ; list) you will type during free-text entry. It is matched without regard to capitals, so NBS and nbs are the same shorthand. A shorthand can be up to 64 characters long.
  • Expands to is the figure or figures it inserts, in order, up to 64 figures. You build them exactly the way you build a normal figure, with the same options and the same checks.

A shorthand also works with a beat count and in a ; list:

  • 16 pbs and pbs (16) add the shorthand with 16 beats. The count is applied when the shorthand is a single figure. A shorthand for several figures is added exactly as saved and the count is ignored, since there is no one figure to give it to. A token that includes a number, such as 3 swings, is matched as typed, before any count is looked for. A count follows the same rules as on any figure line: more than the 64 beats a figure can take (pbs (100)) keeps the whole line as an unrecognized figure, as you typed it, and a count of five digits or more (pbs (10000)) is kept in the figure's note, with the beats the shorthand was saved with.
  • pbs; ladies chain adds the shorthand's figures, then the next figure. Each part can have its own count. As with any ; list, if one part is neither a shorthand nor a figure the app understands, the whole line is added as one unrecognized figure instead.

The same counts work on the shorthands for your own figures and for combinations, described below, whether typed into the free-text box or into the Move box of a row you are changing. 16 zm and zm (16) add your figure with 16 beats, in place of the beats the shorthand was saved with, since it is one figure, and a count over 64 keeps the line as an unrecognized figure, as above. A count typed with a combination is ignored: it is added with the beats it was saved with. A shorthand for a plain figure with the same token as the one left after the count is taken off wins, as it does for a whole line.

They also work as one part of a ; list, mixed with shorthands of any kind and with ordinary figures: zm; ladies chain, ladies chain; zm and 16 zm; ladies chain each add a row per part, in the order typed, in the free-text box or in place of the row whose Move box you typed into. Each part takes its own count as above. A combination is one row and counts as one part, however many figures it holds. A list can have at most 64 parts. As with the shorthands above, it is all or nothing: if one part is not a shorthand, a figure the app understands or a shorthand that can be used, the whole line is added as one unrecognized figure instead.

Shorthands are your own; each one has to be unique, and the app tells you if you reuse a token that is already taken. Shorthands are matched before the app tries to read a line as a figure, so a shorthand named for something the app already understands, such as swing, replaces that reading every time you type it. The editor warns you when your shorthand is such a word, but still lets you save it. The app keeps up to 500 of these shorthands; once you have that many, New shorthand tells you so, and you can add another after deleting one. Deleting a shorthand offers Undo, which puts it back where it was unless you have given its token to another shorthand since. Shorthands only apply while Free-text entry is on.

With Custom Figures on, the same screen also lists Shorthands for your own figures. Choose New shorthand for one of your figures, type the shorthand, pick one of your own figures, a combination or a new figure, and choose the value for each of its parameters; for a new figure, its beats too (left as they are, they follow the figure's own default beats). Typing that shorthand as a whole line adds a row for your figure with those values, as if you had picked it: a combination becomes the figures it stands for when you save the dance, and a new figure stays one figure in your wording. Its token is unique among all your shorthands, of every kind. If its figure has been deleted, or changed so that it no longer accepts the values, the shorthand says so and typing it is read as an ordinary line. Turning Custom Figures off hides these shorthands and stops them working; it doesn't delete them. Each of the two kinds, for combinations and for new figures, keeps up to 500 of its own, separate from the 500 figure shorthands.

A shorthand of any kind works in an existing figure's Move box too: with Free-text entry on, type it there and press Enter, and the figure is replaced by what the shorthand stands for, in the same place in the dance. If the shorthand can't be used (its figure or combination has been deleted, or no longer accepts the values), what you typed is read as an ordinary line instead, the same as for any other text typed there.

Adjust a figure

Select a figure to open it. What you can change depends on the move:

  • Parameters — most moves have a few (which role, how far, which way round). Common ones show straight away; choose More options to see the rest, and Fewer options to fold them back.
  • Who — on an English figure, the dancers it names, any number of them: tap the field to open the list, check each group (type to narrow the list), and the field reads them in the order you checked them, as in "twos, threes". Everyone stands alone, so checking it clears the others. Unchecking them all leaves it not stated.
  • Beats — every figure has a beat count you can raise or lower.
  • Progression — mark the figure the dance progresses on. Moves that can carry the progression say so.
  • Add note — a short note attached to that figure alone.

A figure 8 or a poussette can go a quarter, half, three quarter or full way, or other amount…: picking that shows an amount, starting at one and a half, that you can step from 1¼ to 2½. The figure then reads with it, as in ones 1½ figure 8 up between twos, and a figure 8 counts 16 beats for each full amount. One saved by an earlier version as "other" with no amount reads an unstated amount of figure 8 and asks you to choose the amount; nothing is filled in for you. Your own figures and the per-figure defaults in Settings don't offer "other", because they have no amount to go with it.

Pass by and pass through are one figure under two names, and both are in the move list. Each says who passes, in which direction, by which shoulder, and its beats; Pass type under More options switches between the two. When you add one to a dance, choose who passes and the direction before you save: until you do, the figure says so under its fields and the dance doesn't save. A pass saved earlier, or imported, with either one unstated saves as it is.

A figure 8 also has a Double switch. Turned on, the first pair leads the figure 8 and the other pair casts the mirror way round the outside, as in ones lead a double half figure 8 up between twos, twos cast down the outside (down between ones, ones cast up the outside for down, and twos cast to follow for across). With no direction chosen there is nothing to mirror, so no cast is written. It changes the wording only, not the beat count.

A figure whose move this version of the app does not know — one added by a newer version, for example — is shown read-only, with a plain explanation. Its data is kept untouched, it edits normally again once the app knows the move, and you can still reorder and delete it in the meantime.

Unrecognized figure

An unrecognized figure has two fields: Unrecognized figure text and Beats.

The Beats field sets how many beats the unrecognized figure occupies — use it the same way you would for any other figure.

The Unrecognized figure text field also accepts two formatting marks, with buttons for both:

  • *text* for bold
  • _text_ for underline

As you type, the app styles what it recognizes: move names get a dotted underline, role terms are underlined, and discouraged terms are struck through. The styling is only a hint: it does not change your text while you type. Role words are rewritten when you save, as described next.

Role words you type here (such as "Larks" or "Leads") are saved in a neutral form, shown in your own dialect, and found by role searches. They come back in lower case: "Larks chain wide" shows as "larks chain wide", and "LADIES chain" shows as "robins chain" under Larks and Robins. (If you turn on Capitalize figures in Settings, the first letter is shown capitalized.) Unrecognized figure text saved before this change updates the next time you save the dance while the same dialect is active; text typed in a different dialect may need a manual edit. The same rewriting applies to the everyday and older role words "man", "men", "woman", "women", "lady", "ladies", "gent" and "gents" inside unrecognized figure text (for example, "the man and lady swing"), and to the role names of the built-in dialects ("lark", "robin", "gentlespoon", "ladle" and their plurals). They are saved as role terms and shown in your dialect's words. Words that are also move words are not turned into role terms, here and in figure notes. The move name "mad robin" is kept as the move name and saved in lower case, so "Mad Robin twice" comes back as "mad robin twice". If your dialect uses Leads and Follows, "lead" and "follow" used as verbs ("Ones lead down the hall", "follow your partner up the hall") are kept exactly as you typed them. "Leads chain" and "the lead on the left" still use the role. Text saved by an earlier version is not repaired automatically.

Your own figures

With Custom Figures on (see Settings), the figures you built for the dance's form, in the Glossary or from Settings ▸ Experimental ▸ Custom Figures, are offered when you choose a figure's move, listed first and marked with a person icon (announced as Custom by screen readers). They are found by their name or by any of their other names. A figure you built has one of two kinds, and each works differently in a dance. (The Glossary can also hold Term entries, which are never offered in a dance.) You can start a figure of your own from one that comes with the app, and rename it or switch to the app's version if a later release adds a figure with the same name; see Glossary and custom figures.

A combination

A combination is offered for a figure in the list itself, not inside a meanwhile, modifier or sequence group.

A combination you pick stays one row while you edit: it shows its name and the value you chose for each of its parameters, and opening it lets you change those values (only values the combination accepts are offered), mark the progression, and add a note. It says how many figures, and how many beats, it becomes.

When you save the dance, the row is replaced by the figures it stands for, filled in with your values, as the combination is defined at that moment, so an edit made in the Glossary since you picked it applies. Your note goes on the first of them and the progression mark on the last. After that they are ordinary figures: the saved dance does not remember that they came from one of yours, and changing or deleting your combination later does not change it.

If your combination has been deleted, has changed so that it can no longer be expanded, or no longer accepts the values on the row, the row says so, and saving keeps it as an unrecognized figure with its name, its beats, and the row's note and progression mark. The values you chose for its parameters are not kept. A row that is one of your combinations cannot be grouped as a meanwhile, modifier or sequence. Turning Custom Figures off stops them being offered, but a row you already picked is still expanded when you save.

Versions of the app before 0.10.0 can't read a figure 8 or poussette with an "other amount", or a figure 8 marked double (see Adjust a figure). On a device that hasn't been updated, a combination of yours that uses one is marked Needs repair and can't be picked or used by a shorthand, though it is kept as it is. Repairing it there means changing or removing that figure, which drops the amount or the double, and the change then syncs to your other devices, so update the device instead. Dances, backups and shared files that hold such a figure keep it on an older version too, which reads an amount as "other figure 8" without the amount, and a double as an ordinary figure 8.

A new figure

A new figure is offered for a figure in the list and inside a meanwhile, modifier or sequence group.

A new figure you pick stays one figure, in the dance and once it is saved. It starts with its defaults and its default beats. Opening it lets you choose the value of each of its parameters and its beats (inside a meanwhile or modifier group, the group's beats count; inside a sequence, its own do), mark the progression, and add a note. It reads in the dance with its own wording, filled in with your values.

The dance keeps following your figure: when you change its wording in the Glossary, every dance that uses it reads the new way, on this device. Saving the dance also stores how it reads at that moment, which is what a device without your figure, or with an older version of the app, shows. If you change that line on such a device, it becomes an ordinary unrecognized figure there and stops following your figure.

If your figure has been deleted, the row says so and keeps the line it had. Deleting a figure from the Glossary changes each dance that uses it to that line, as an unrecognized figure. Turning Custom Figures off stops new figures being offered, but a dance that already uses one still reads with its wording.

Reorder, cut, and duplicate

Each figure row has a drag handle and an actions menu. From the keyboard, Tab to the row's ⋮ button and press Space or Enter to open it; the arrow keys move between actions, Enter runs one, and Escape closes the menu:

ActionWhat it does
Move up / Move downShifts the figure one place
CutLifts the figure out, ready to place elsewhere
DuplicateAdds an identical figure
Group with next as meanwhileJoins it to the next figure as simultaneous action

After Cut, the figure keeps its normal colours but gets a dashed outline and a Cut label, so it is clear which figure is waiting, and a banner names it and Paste here points appear between the rows — before the first figure, after any figure, and at the end of the list — so you can drop it exactly where you want. Every move, duplication, and deletion is announced for screen readers, and deletions can be undone.

Meanwhile figures

Some dances have two things happening at once — the ones on the ends do one thing while the middles do another. Choose Group with next as meanwhile and the two figures become a meanwhile group, labeled with how many sides it has and sharing one set of beats. Until you type a count for the group, its beats follow its sides: the longest side's, since the sides are danced at the same time. A modifier group follows its Core, and a sequence adds up its figures (see Sequence figures), unless an English sequence states its own Bars for all (see English dances are timed in bars) or a square's states its own Beats for all (see One count for a square's calls). This holds however the group was made: grouped, added from the Add menu, typed with || or while, or in a shorthand.

You can also choose Add meanwhile from the list's Add menu to create the group first. It starts with your Meanwhile defaults (see Start new dances the way you work), or two stand-still sides if you have not set any. If you have cleared those defaults you get two blank sides, and a single default side is paired with one blank one. The group is saved with the dance once at least two of its sides are filled in; until then it is not counted in the Total (see Keep an eye on the beats).

Inside the group, each side is labeled Side 1, Side 2, and so on, and has its own controls to move up, move down, or Remove this side. Add side adds another side, up to six.

A note you add to one side, or to one figure of a modifier or sequence group, is shown in brackets after that figure wherever the dance is shown: in the list, on the dance's page, in Perform and when printed. For example, robins allemande right once (1 1/2) while larks orbit. If you choose Customize wording for this dance on the group itself, your wording replaces the whole line, its sides' notes included.

Any group, a meanwhile, a modifier or a sequence, can also have a name. Type it in Group title (optional) at the top of the group, and the group's line starts with it, followed by a dash: a sequence titled Flutterwheel reads Flutterwheel - robins allemande right ½ and neighbor star promenade ½. Use it for a figure you call by one name but write as its parts. The name is shown wherever the line is, comes before your own wording if you customize it, and is found by search. Role words in it, such as larks or gents, follow each reader's dialect, as they do in a note. A group inside another group has a title field of its own. Leave the field empty for no name.

Modifier figures

Use Group with next as modifier when one figure describes the main action and the next describes how it is performed. The first figure is labeled Core and the figures after it Modifier, in order. Add modifier creates a modifier group from your Modifier defaults.

Sequence figures

A sequence is two to four figures danced one after another and written as one line: ones balance and swing, or twos box the gnat, pull by right, and orbit clockwise ½. On its own a sequence reads just like the same figures in separate rows. It is for a run of figures that has to stand in one place, most often one side of a meanwhile group: ones balance and swing while twos box the gnat, pull by right, and orbit clockwise ½. When a figure repeats the dancers of the one before it, the line names them once.

Choose Group with next as sequence, Add sequence from the Add menu, or type sequence in a figure's Move box to make it the first figure of a new sequence. Each figure in the group is labeled Figure 1, Figure 2, and so on, and keeps its own Beats: the group's beats are their total, shown at the top (an English sequence can instead state its own Bars for all; see English dances are timed in bars, and a square's its own Beats for all; see One count for a square's calls). A sequence must fit in the phrase it starts in: 16 beats in the usual structure, and where phrases differ in length, such as an A of 4 bars and a B of 8, the length of the one it starts in. It may start partway through that phrase and run over its end, as long as it is no longer than the phrase. So Group with next as sequence is only offered when the two figures fit, and Add next figure goes away once the figures fill that phrase or there are four of them (with Bars for all or Beats for all, only figures with a count of their own count toward the phrase). Changing a figure already in the sequence, its move or its beats, is not stopped, and neither is a change to the phrase structure or to the figures before it, which can move it into a shorter phrase: if the sequence ends up longer than the phrase it starts in, the group and the dance's warnings say so, and you can still save it.

Convert to sequence, Convert to meanwhile and Convert to modifier change a group from one kind to another when its figures fit the new kind. A sequence turned into a meanwhile or modifier group then follows its figures' beats as that kind of group does.

You can put a group inside a group of another kind — a sequence inside a meanwhile, or a meanwhile inside a modifier, for example — one level deep. The app does not allow a group inside another of the same kind, or nesting any deeper.

English dances are timed in bars

An English (ECD) dance is timed in bars, as English callers time it. Each figure's length is entered and shown in Bars: 2, or 1.5 (or 1½) for a bar and a half. Half a bar is taken only where it is a whole number of beats, so not in triple time: in a tune with three beats to the bar, the field asks for whole bars instead. The app still works in beats underneath. A bar holds the beats of the dance's Meter: 2/2, 2/4, 6/8 and 6/4 have two, 3/2, 3/4, 9/8 and 9/4 three, 4/4, 12/8 and 12/4 four, so in 3/2 two bars are 6 beats. With no meter the phrase structure says, so 4*8*3 (four 8-bar phrases of three beats) makes 2 bars 6 beats too. A tune whose phrases have different numbers of beats to the bar (A:2*4*3 + B:1*8*2, an A in 3/4 and a B in 6/8) counts each bar by the phrase it starts in, whatever the Meter says, and from where its figure starts: the two bars at the end of an A and the first two of a B are 3 + 3 + 2

  • 2 beats, and the field shows them as 4 bars. A count that is not a whole number of half bars where it falls shows in beats. The count is stored in beats when you enter it, so a figure that ends up in another phrase, because a figure before it was lengthened or removed, is read there: in the bars of that phrase, or in beats where it no longer comes to whole or half bars. The dance's page and Perform show bars too, and so do the dance's warnings, its calling card, and its figures when you share, copy or print it, alone or with a program. A calling card prints bare numbers, so if any count on it is not a whole number of half bars, the whole card stays in beats.

An English sequence can also take its bars as a whole, as in "A, B and C in bars 1–4". Fill in Bars for all at the top of the sequence, and leave the Bars of any figure in it empty to give that figure no timing of its own. Leave Bars for all empty and the sequence adds up its figures' bars, as a contra sequence does; emptying it gives each figure with no bars its move's usual length. A figure that leaves the sequence, by ungrouping it or turning it into another kind of group, takes its usual length too. If every figure has bars of its own and they don't add up to Bars for all, or the figures with bars already fill it, the sequence and the dance's warnings say so. Changing the dance to a contra puts its sequences back to adding up their figures, and a figure with no bars takes its move's usual length; Undo puts them back. Changing it to a square keeps each span, counted in beats there (see One count for a square's calls).

One count for a square's calls

A square's caller often gives one count for two calls, as in "forward again and swing the opposite (8)". Group the calls as a sequence, fill in Beats for all at the top of it, and leave the Beats of any call in it empty to give that call no timing of its own. It works as an English sequence's Bars for all does, in beats: left empty, the sequence adds up its calls' beats; if every call has beats of its own and they don't add up to Beats for all, or the calls with beats already fill it, the sequence and the dance's warnings say so. Changing the dance to a contra puts its sequences back to adding up their calls, and Undo puts them back.

The "forward again" in such a line is a forward and back with Go back turned off: it reads "forward", and on its own it counts 4 beats rather than

  1. A promenade goes counter-clockwise unless you set its Direction to clockwise, which is the only way it then says. A wrong-way promenade is a clockwise one.

If a dance holds figures of another type than its own, for example English figures in a dance you have just changed to contra, Warnings says how many. They are kept and shown as they are, but the figure picker offers only the dance's own type's figures. Imports still read a square dance's lines as contra figures, so a square imported that way shows this warning; a stand still row, such as those a new dance starts with, isn't counted.

Keep an eye on the beats

Under the figure list the app shows a running Total against what the dance's phrase structure expects, and says Over by or Under by when the two disagree. It is a nudge, not a rule — you can save a dance whose beats do not add up, and sometimes that is the honest transcription. A saved dance whose beats do not add up shows the same warning above its figures on the dance's page, because the section labels (A1, A2, B1, B2) are then worked out from the wrong total. Perform and printing do not show it.

In a square dance, Warnings, in the editor and on the dance's page, also names a figure that starts in one phrase and runs past its end, such as a 16-beat promenade starting halfway through A1: the calls of each phrase should fill it exactly. A figure that starts at the top of a phrase and fills whole phrases, such as a 32-beat promenade across A1 and A2, is fine.

The Total counts only rows that will be saved. A group that is not complete yet, such as a meanwhile group with one side filled in, is left out, and so is a row with no move. A row with no move but a note is saved as an unrecognized figure holding the note, so the note is not lost.

A row that Save would leave out says so, Not complete, so Save leaves it out, and is listed under Warnings by its figure number. That is a group (meanwhile, modifier or sequence) with fewer than two of its figures filled in, such as a sequence just added with two blank steps; a modifier group with no Core; a group holding such a group; or a row with no move that still holds something other than a note, such as a walkthrough step. Fill it in, or remove it, and the warning goes. A blank row, with nothing chosen or typed, is not listed: there is nothing in it to lose.

Details, notes, and credits

Details

Under the Title, everything else in Details is in one panel, Credits, formation and music, so the figures are not pushed down the page by fields you set once. Folded, it shows a short summary instead, such as Pat Shaw · English (ECD) · Duple minor · Starts improper · 3/2 · G minor; tap it to open it, and tap its header again to fold it. It starts folded when you edit a dance, and when you start one on a phone; it starts open when you start one on a wider screen. If Save finds a mistake in one of its fields, it opens to show you. Inside, fields sit side by side where there is room and one under another where there is not.

  • Title — required. On a new dance, the keyboard's Next (or Enter) takes you straight to the figures: it opens the first figure still to fill, ready for you to type its move, or starts the first one. On a dance you're editing, or a new one whose figures are all set, the key is Done.

  • Authors — the author or authors of the dance. Tap the field to open the list of people: check each author, type in the search box to narrow the list, or choose Create to add someone new. The pencil beside a name edits that person's details. Interpreted by works the same way, and so does Tags, without the pencil.

  • For an English (ECD) dance, the credits for its original and its interpretation come next, each as names with a year beside them: Authors with Composed, then Interpreted by with Interpreted in. Many English dances were published long ago and reconstructed later, as in "Walsh, 1731, Pat Shaw version": put the original's author or publisher in Authors and its year in Composed, and the person who reconstructed it, and the year, in the other two. On a wide screen the two lines sit side by side; on a narrow screen, or with large text, they stack, and each year goes under its names. Interpreted by draws on the same list of people as Authors. For other types the dates and Interpreted by are in More details, where a date can also have a month and day. The dance's detail view and its Perform card show each year beside its names, such as Pat Shaw (1951) and Interpreted by Cecil Sharp (1916); they show only the year of a date that has a month and day.

  • Type — Contra, English (ECD), or Square. It sets the icon on the dance's row in your collection and what the Type filter finds. A new dance starts with your default type (see Start new dances the way you work); change it here for any one dance.

  • Formation and Formation detail — the shape the dance is danced in, plus anything worth adding in words. An English dance offers English formations, such as duple minor, triple minor and three-couple longways, with a Starts improper chip under them: tap it on for a dance that starts improper, and leave it off for one that starts proper. A square offers Square, Circle for a big-circle dance such as a running set, and Other. Changing the type to one whose list doesn't have the dance's formation starts the formation over: on Duple minor for English, Improper for contra, and Square for a square. A formation both lists have, such as triple minor between contra and English or circle between English and square, is kept. Undo brings the old formation back.

  • Progression — how the dance progresses, such as Single. It sits beside Type and Formation where there is room.

  • Rating — your own star rating, with Clear rating to unset it. It sits beside the Starts improper and Mixer chips where there is room.

  • Mixer — tap this chip on when the dance is a mixer: one where you change partners each time through. It's kept separate from the formation, because a mixer can be danced in many shapes and not every circle is a mixer.

  • Square fields — a square dance shows five more fields here:

    • Kind of square: Main part (the figure danced several times through), Break, or Patter.
    • Free form: turn this on for patter that follows no phrasing. The figures then get no phrase labels (A1, A2 and so on), nothing checks that they fill each phrase, the phrase structure is hidden, and the figure list shows only the total beats. Patter starts with it on and the other kinds with it off; you can change either. The phrase structure you had is kept for when you turn it off.
    • Repeat order: Heads twice, then sides twice, Heads and sides alternating, Four times, everyone active, or Other, which opens a field to describe it.
    • New partner: who each dancer ends with each time through, such as Corner or Partners stay the same. Other opens a field here too. Mixer still says whether partners change; this says to whom.
    • Singing call: put the song under Tunes.

    The dance's page shows each one you've set. Another type of dance keeps them in More details, so changing the type and back loses nothing.

  • Phrase structure — leave it blank for the standard A1 A2 B1 B2, or write your own (the field suggests 6*8*2 as an example). The app checks that what you write is a valid structure, and uses it for the beat count above. A contra or square dance also shows it as rows above the field, one for each run of equal phrases: Phrases (how many), Bars per phrase and Beats per bar. 8*8*2 is one row, 8 phrases of 8 bars with 2 beats in a bar; 3*8*2 + 1*4*2 is two, three 8-bar phrases and then one of 4 bars; a slip jig has 3 beats in a bar. Add row adds another run, and × removes a row. A line under the rows lists the labels the phrases get and the beats in all: 8 phrases are labeled A1 A2 B1 B2 C1 C2 D1 D2, so a tune played twice carries on to C and D. Each number is 1 to 1000; while one is left empty or past that, the dance keeps its last valid structure, and Save saves that. The rows and the field show the same structure, so you can use either; a row with a number missing waits for it, and the structure stays as it was. While what you type in the field can't be read as a structure, or has phrase names (which only an English dance keeps), the rows stay as they were and are dimmed until it can. A dance that opens with a structure that can't be read shows no rows until it can.

  • Phrases (English dances) — choose Name the phrases to give the tune's parts their own names instead: one row each for A, B and C, with its Bars, its Beats per bar and whether it plays Once or Twice, the repeat straight after it. A=4 played twice and B=8 played once labels the figures A1, A2 and B; A=4, B=4 and C=8 each once labels them A, B and C. A phrase's beats to the bar start as the dance's Meter gives them (a meter naming several meters gives each new phrase the first phrase's), and you can set each phrase's own, such as 3 for an A in 3/4 and 2 for a B in 6/8: the dance is then counted in bars phrase by phrase (see English dances are timed in bars). Changing the Meter to one that now states other beats to the bar sets every phrase to them, keeping each one's bars, but only while the phrases all have the same beats to the bar: phrases that differ are never rewritten from the Meter. A Meter that still states the same beats (a word added after 3/4), or names two meters (3/4 and 6/8), leaves them as they are too. Name the phrases on a structure that differs keeps each part's own beats where its parts group into named phrases. Each phrase has 1 to 1000 bars and 1 to 12 beats to the bar (12 is the most a meter can name); while one is left empty or past that, the dance keeps its last valid phrases, and Save saves those. That includes a phrase typed earlier with more than 12 beats to the bar: it shows the error and its text is kept until you set that phrase's beats (or change the Meter so that it states other beats, which sets every phrase's, as above). Remove every phrase to go back to unnamed phrases: the standard A1 A2 B1 B2, counted in the dance's meter. You can also type names into the Phrase structure field (A:2*4*2 + B:1*8*2); only an English dance can be saved with them. Printed cards, PDFs and copied text show named phrases the way English callers write them: A=4 played twice and B=8 once prints as AAB (A 4 bars, B 8 bars), with your language's punctuation (in Japanese, AAB（A 4小節、B 8小節）).

  • Meter and Key — the tune's written meter, such as 3/2, and its key, such as G minor, in your own words. They sit here for an English dance and in More details for other types. The dance's detail view and its Perform card show them on one line, such as Meter: 6/8 · Key: G minor.

  • Barnes reference — for an English dance only: where the tune is printed in the Barnes books, book and page in one line, such as BIII p. 92, or NiB when it is not in them. It sits beside Meter and Key, or under them on a narrow screen. Changing a dance to another type clears it when you save. The dance's detail view shows it under Barnes books, and its Perform card as Barnes: BIII p. 92.

Notes

  • Calling notes — how you call it, what to watch for.
  • Hook — one line on why you would call this dance. Printed and shared dance cards can show it, and the dance's published sources, once you turn on Hook and Sources under Settings ▸ Defaults ▸ Shared dance fields.
  • Walkthrough — the step-by-step teach.

Walkthrough

You can write the walkthrough as free prose, or build it from your figures.

Each figure can carry an Add walkthrough step description. What you write there is saved as your default for that figure and reused wherever it appears — so the way you teach a hey is written once and follows you into every dance with a hey.

Two things follow from that:

  • Fill from snippets assembles a walkthrough from the saved snippets of the dance's figures. If it would overwrite something, the app asks first. If none of the figures has a snippet yet, it says so rather than emptying the field.
  • If you edit a step so it no longer matches your saved snippet, the app asks Update your saved snippet? — choose Use everywhere to update the default, or Just this dance to keep the change local.

Your whole library of snippets lives at Settings ▸ Dialect ▸ Dance details & shorthands ▸ Walkthrough snippets, where you can review and edit them. Each snippet is shown in the dialect of its figure's dance form, so an English figure's snippet reads in your English dialect. Editing one there updates the default used everywhere.

Author and source details are shared

Choose to edit an author's details and you get Author details — name, website, email, location, notes, and whether they are deceased. The dialog is explicit about two things, and both matter:

  • If you rename an author to a name another author already has, the app offers to merge them into one, listing any details it would drop because the two disagree. Your work is kept while you type

The editor keeps a draft as you work. If the app closes before you save — a crash, a flat battery, a phone that decides to reboot — the next time you open that dance the app offers you the draft back:

Unsaved draft — You have an unsaved draft for this dance. Would you like to restore it?

The prompt also says when the draft was saved. If the dance has changed since then — a tag or rating set on several dances at once, a change from Device Sync, or Re-check unrecognized figures — it says so: restoring the draft puts back the dance as the draft has it, so those later changes are replaced.

Choose Restore to pick up where you left off, or Discard to start from the last saved version.

The draft is written about half a second after you stop typing, and at once when you switch away from the app on a phone or tablet, or close it on a computer, so even the last few keystrokes are kept. Drafts stay on your device; they are never uploaded, shared, or included in exports.

If the app cannot write the draft — the device is out of storage space, for example — a small cloud-with-a-slash icon appears in the top bar of the dance editor and the program editor, and its tooltip says Draft not saved — your latest changes are not being kept yet. Your edits stay on screen and you can keep working, but nothing is being kept in case the app closes. Free up space, or save the dance or program now. The icon disappears by itself the next time a draft is written. The app also notes the first such failure each time it runs in Diagnostics (by error type only, never your text).

Each new dance keeps its own draft. If a new dance you never saved left one behind, the next new dance you start offers it back by its title — You have an unsaved draft of another new dance — so you can Restore it and carry on with that dance, or Discard it and start fresh. A new dance you start from a program slot is that slot's dance, so it does not offer one; the draft waits for the next new dance you start from the collection.

A restored draft keeps the beat counts you typed by hand as typed, the same as undo and redo do, and figure notes come back in the dialect you use now, even if you changed dialects since the draft was saved.

Leaving without saving

If you try to leave with unsaved changes, the app asks Discard changes? — Keep editing (the highlighted button) returns you to the dance, and Discard (in red) leaves it as it was last saved.

The editors for figure shorthands, custom themes, venues and custom fields ask the same question when you leave one with changes: by Back, the back arrow, or a tap outside the venue or custom-field sheet. Those two sheets do not close when swiped down, and their Cancel button closes them without asking.

Save and delete

Save writes the dance and returns you to where you came from; the change shows in your collection straight away. If a save fails, the app says so and leaves your work in the editor so you can try again.

Delete dance (in the top bar, on an existing dance) removes the dance. As everywhere else in the app, deletion is a soft delete with an Undo offered right away (if you turned on Confirm before delete in Settings, the app asks first) — see Keep your collection tidy.

Start new dances the way you work

If most of your dances share a shape, set that shape once. Settings ▸ Defaults ▸ Dance-authoring defaults decides what a New dance starts as:

  • Type, Formation, and Progression — what a new dance begins with.
  • Default phrase structure — seeded into new dances; blank means the standard 4×16 (A1 A2 B1 B2). It is set as the same rows as a contra or square dance's Phrase structure, with the field under them. Phrase names (A:2*8*2 + B:1*8*2, for English dances) go in the field; the rows don't show for a default that has them, and are dimmed if you type them.
  • Starting figures — the figures a new dance starts with. It defaults to eight stand stills of eight beats each, or to no figures while Free-text entry is on; clear it for a completely blank dance.
  • Meanwhile defaults and Modifier defaults — the figures a new meanwhile or modifier group starts with.
  • Move defaults — your preferred parameter values, applied whenever you insert that move, whether you pick it or type it. These override the move's built-in defaults, but not a value you type in the line, and you can still change any parameter on the figure afterwards.
  • Aggressively recompute figure beats — off by default, so a beat count you typed yourself is left alone, except that switching a balance option on or off adds or removes 4 beats. Turn it on and changing a figure's move, or an option that affects its timing, recalculates its beats straight away, even over a count you typed.

Every one of these is only a starting point; you can change any of them on any dance.

Fix figures an import could not read

Imported dances sometimes arrive with figures kept as plain text because the app could not recognize them at the time. As the app's figure recognition improves, those can often be upgraded.

In Settings ▸ General ▸ Import, find Re-check unrecognized figures and choose Re-check…. The app scans your collection and shows you what it could upgrade before anything changes: how many figures, in how many dances, and which ones. You confirm, and only then does anything happen.

Your tags, ratings, notes, and everything else on each dance are kept exactly as they are — only figures that now recognize a known move are replaced. If there is nothing to upgrade, the app tells you that and leaves your collection alone.

A figure is upgraded only to one of its own dance's type: an English dance's lines to English figures, and a contra dance's to contra figures. The app can't read square calls from text yet, so a square dance's lines stay as they are, rather than becoming contra figures.

A dance whose stored figures the app cannot read at all is left out of the scan rather than upgraded, and its stored text is not touched. The dance's own page tells you when its figures cannot be read.
