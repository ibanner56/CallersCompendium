# Perform mode

[Perform mode](./glossary.md#perform-mode) is the app's stage-ready calling view. This
guide covers calling a single [dance](./glossary.md#dance) or a whole
[program](./glossary.md#program), sizing the text so it is readable from a distance,
moving through your evening, keeping time, and making changes on the fly without
losing your place.

> **Finding your way around these words.** On-screen buttons and screens are
> written in **bold** — like **Perform this dance** and **Perform this program**.
> The first time a dance term appears it links to
> [App terms](./glossary.md), so you can get a plain-language definition
> without losing your place.

New to the app? Start with the [Getting started guide](./getting-started.md).

## Enter Perform mode

Perform mode is not a navigation tab — you step into it when it is time to call,
and step back out deliberately when you are done. On a phone or tablet it
fills the whole screen: the status bar, and on Android the navigation bar, hide
while you perform and come back when you exit. On Android, swipe in from the
edge to see them for a moment. On a computer, Perform opens in the app window, and **Full screen**
takes it over the whole display (see [Set the stage](#set-the-stage)).

- To call a **single dance**, open it from your
  [collection](./collection.md#what-you-can-do-with-a-dance) and choose
  **Perform this dance**.
- To call a **whole evening**, choose **Perform this program** on the
  program's summary. The program builder is for editing and has no Perform.

When you exit, the app takes you back to wherever you came from.

## Read the card

Perform mode shows one dance at a time in very large type, with the
[figures](./glossary.md#figure) grouped by section (A1, A2, B1, B2) and set in a
typeface built to be read from a distance.

Above the figures, the card shows the dance's title, its authors with the year
it was composed, who interpreted it and when (if anyone did), its formation
and level, the tune's meter and key on one line when the dance has either,
and where the tune is in the Barnes books (such as *Barnes: BIII p. 92*) when
you have filled that in; below them come the dance's calling notes. The figure
where the progression happens is marked with an icon labeled *Progression*. Break and
note slots in a program get a card of their own, with the text in the same
large type.

Your [dialect](./dialects.md) for the dance's type is applied — or the
program's own dialect, if you gave it one (see [Programs](./programs.md)) — so
the card speaks in your words.
To see the shared wording, choose **Canonical** under **Switch dialect** below
(that section says how long the change lasts). Figure
detail you recorded shows here too: when a swing ends facing somewhere other
than the usual "in"/across — up or down the hall, or out of the set — the card
notes that ending so you can cue it.

## Set the stage

A row of controls along the top shapes the calling view itself. On a tablet or a
desktop they all sit there as buttons. On a phone, only **Switch dialect** and
the **Stage theme** toggle stay out — the rest tuck into a **More actions** menu
so the toolbar can't crowd a narrow screen. Wherever a control lives it does the
same thing, and every toggle says which state it is in rather than relying on
how it looks.

**Switch dialect.** Changes your active dialect for the type of the dance on
screen (contra, English, or square), across the whole app — handy when tonight's
hall uses different role names. The change stays after you leave
Perform: later screens, PDFs and shared text use that dialect too, and it syncs to
your other devices. **Canonical** is one of the choices, for when a dancer or
another caller asks what a figure is in the shared wording; switch back the same
way when you are done. If the program has its own dialect, the switch changes
the wording for this time in Perform only: your active dialect stays as it was,
and the program's dialect is back the next time you open Perform. See
[Dialect](./dialects.md#switch-dialect-on-the-fly).

**Stage theme.** Perform opens on a **high-contrast dark-stage theme** by default,
built for strong legibility (a contrast ratio of at least 7 to 1) under stage
lighting. Toggle it off to fall back to your usual app theme. Its tooltip reads
*Stage theme on — tap to use app theme* or *Stage theme off — tap for dark stage*,
so there is no guessing, and it stays where you put it — next time too.

**Auto-size text to screen.** Out of the box this is on: the card scales so the
current dance's full text fits the screen, recomputing whenever you move to a
new dance or slot, rotate the device, or resize the window. So the text is as
large as it can be while still fitting. It never shrinks the figure lines, their
notes or the dance's status line below the size of ordinary text in the rest of
the app, which follows your device's text-size setting: a dance too long to fit
at that size scrolls instead of getting smaller. The beat count beside each
figure has a fixed largest size, so it doesn't crowd the figure: at a large
device text size it can be smaller than your usual text. It starts however you set **Auto-size Perform cards** in
[Settings ▸ Program ▸ Perform mode](./settings.md#perform-mode), and the in-view
toggle flips it until you leave Perform.

**A− and A+.** The **Decrease text size** and **Increase text size** controls step
the size down and up, starting large with no upper limit. Using either one
switches auto-size off, because you have told the app what size you want. The
size you pick is remembered for next time; auto-size goes back to your Settings
choice the next time you open Perform.

**Tap tempo** opens a silent, visual metronome: tap out the beat on the large
target to see the tempo in beats per minute and a pulse you can follow or show
the band. **Show walkthrough** — which appears when the dance has a
walkthrough written — lays it over the card until you close it.

**Full screen.** On a computer, this takes the window over the whole display,
covering the taskbar or Dock, and back again. The keyboard does the same:
**F11** on Windows and Linux, **Ctrl-Cmd-F** on macOS. **Esc** also leaves full
screen. It never leaves Perform, which asks first unless you have turned that
off (see [Leaving Perform](#leaving-perform)). When you exit Perform,
the window goes back to how it was when you came in. If you close the app while
it is full screen, it opens in its usual window next time. Phones and tablets
have no button for this, because Perform is always full screen there.

**Caller notes.** When you perform a program, a slot's caller note appears above
the dance title, labeled *Caller note*. To hide these, turn off **Show caller
notes when performing a program** in
[Settings ▸ Program ▸ Perform mode](./settings.md#perform-mode). The note is for
your eyes only: it does not change the dance's own calling notes, search, or
exports.

## The screen stays awake

While you are in Perform mode, the app keeps the screen awake, so a propped-up
tablet will not dim or sleep partway through a dance. The moment you exit, the
screen is free to sleep as normal again.

## Move through a program

When you are performing a program, step through your slots with whichever
control suits you and your setup:

- the **Previous slot** and **Next slot** buttons at the bottom of the screen;
- the **giant edge hit zones** — tap the left edge to go back, the right edge
  to go forward — easy to hit without looking; or
- the **arrow keys** or **Page Up** / **Page Down** on a keyboard, or a
  page-turner pedal that sends them.

When a card is too long to fit, **down** and **Page Down** scroll it first, and
move to the next slot only once you can see the end of the card. **Up** and
**Page Up** scroll back to the top before they go back a slot. Each press moves
most of a screen, so a pedal reads through a long card in a few presses. The
**left** and **right** keys always change slot straight away. A new slot always
opens at its top. A card that fits needs no scrolling, so every key changes slot
at once.

When you perform a single dance, the same **up**, **down**, **Page Up** and
**Page Down** keys scroll a long card, most of a screen at a time. There is no
next slot to move to, so they stop at the top and the end.

When you are calling a program and the walkthrough overlay is open, none of
these change your slot: taps on
the card are absorbed, the **up**, **down**, **Page Up** and **Page Down** keys
scroll the walkthrough itself, and **Esc** closes it — so reading ahead can't
move you off the dance you are calling.

Between the two buttons, **Slot N of M** shows where you are. A dance and its
alternates count as one slot, so stepping forward skips past the alternates.

**Jump to slot** lists the whole program so you can go anywhere at once — useful
if plans change mid-evening. Dances are numbered as in your set list, so a
break takes no number. When the current slot has an
[alt](./glossary.md#alt), **Show alternate** switches the card to it (and on
through any others, then back to the primary). This only changes what you are
looking at; the program itself stays as it is.

If a dance in your program has been deleted, its slot still shows the figures
as long as the dance is in Recently deleted, and both the card title and
**Jump to slot** mark it "(deleted dance)". A dance that no longer exists at all shows "Dance
unavailable" instead; your note for that slot, if you have notes turned on,
appears underneath.

## Keep time through the evening

Perform mode shows timing in a status area so you can pace yourself. For a
single dance, it shows:

- an **elapsed timer** for the dance; and
- a **pause/resume** control for interruptions.

For a program, the status area additionally shows:

- a **running program clock** for the whole evening, labelled **Program**;
- a **per-slot elapsed timer** that resets each time you move to a new slot,
  labelled **Slot**;
- the slot's planned length, when you have set one, shown as **planned N min
  (W:D)** — where W is the walkthrough minutes and D is the dance minutes;
- once the slot's walkthrough minutes have passed, the words **walkthrough
  complete**, with a running-figure icon;
- once the walkthrough and dance minutes together have passed, the word
  **over**, with a timer icon; and
- a **Pause timers** button (**Resume timers** while paused) that freezes both
  clocks for interruptions; and
- when the slot has a walkthrough length, a round **dance-start** button (a
  music note) at the end of the readout.

### Mark when the walkthrough really ended

Walkthroughs rarely run to the clock. When a slot has a walkthrough length, tap
the dance-start button the moment the dancers actually start dancing. Its tooltip
reads "Walkthrough done — start the dance"; once marked it becomes a filled
circle and reads "Dance started — tap to undo". Tap it again to undo a
mis-tap. You can press it early or late, while paused, or after **over** is
already showing.

While a slot is marked:

- **walkthrough complete** follows your mark instead of the clock;
- **over** is counted from your mark plus the dance minutes, not from the start
  of the slot; and
- the readout adds how long the walkthrough actually took against its plan, for
  example **walkthrough 4:10 (+1:10)** (ran over), **(−0:50)** (finished early)
  or **(on plan)**.

If you never tap the button, nothing changes: the cues follow the clock exactly
as described above. The mark belongs to the slot you are on, so it clears when
you move to another slot, swap in an alternate or jump to a slot (the same
moments the per-slot timer restarts), and it survives reordering or inserting
slots around the one you are reading. It is session-only: it is kept when you
leave and re-enter Perform within the same session, but it is never saved to
your program or your dances.

While Perform is open, the timers keep counting when you switch to another
app, such as your music player, or when the device sleeps: coming back shows
the full time that passed. Only **Pause timers** stops them.

Timing is display-only — it helps you keep an eye on the clock but never changes
your program or your dances. The single-dance timer is on by default; to hide
it, turn off **Show timer when performing one dance** in
[Settings ▸ Program ▸ Perform mode](./settings.md#perform-mode).

## Adjust on the fly

Plans change mid-gig. When you are calling a program, **Adjust program** opens a
sheet that lets you make changes without disturbing the card you are reading.
From it you can:

- **reorder the remaining slots** (with a drag handle or move up / move down
  buttons);
- **insert a dance** with **Insert dance from search**;
- **add an ad-hoc note or break**;
- **swap a dance** for another that keeps what matters, with a marker on any
  dance that would break one of the program's
  [event rules](./settings.md#event-rules) (see
  [Swap a dance](#swap-a-dance)); and
- **mark the current slot performed** (choose it again to clear the mark).

An inserted dance and an ad-hoc note both land right after the current slot, so
"play this next" is one action away. Your changes are kept however you close
the sheet — **Done**, swiping it down, tapping outside it, or **Back** — so a
hurried swipe doesn't throw away a reorder or a performed mark. Everything you
change between opening the sheet and closing it is one change: it offers
**Undo**, and for a saved program it is saved right away. The
Undo is still offered for a moment after you leave Perform (for as long as you
need with a screen reader), but only while the program is as the change left
it. So if the program has been changed since, for example edited in the
builder, Undo does nothing except tell you it can no longer undo, and the later
change is kept.

Perform shows the program as it was when you opened it. If the program changes
somewhere else while Perform is open — most often when
[Device Sync](./settings.md#device-sync) brings in an edit from another device
— your next change is usually saved on top of that edit rather than replacing
it. Perform then switches to the newer program and says so: *This program had
changed elsewhere since Perform opened. Perform now shows the latest version,
with your change.* Undo still takes back only your own change.

Sometimes your change can't be put on top, because it would land on the wrong
dance. That happens when, on the other device, a slot you changed (its note,
its performed mark or, with a swap, its dance, or any of these with an
**Undo**) was given another dance, or a slot you swapped was deleted, moved to another group, made or
unmade an alternate, or marked performed. Then none of that change is saved,
and it no longer offers **Undo**. Perform shows the program as it now is, keeps
on screen any change you made after it that is still being saved, and says
*This program changed on another device, so one of your changes wasn't saved.
Check the program and make it again if you still want it.* If you have already
left Perform, the message appears on the screen you returned to instead. A
change you are making in the adjust sheet when this happens, or when a newer
program arrives, is put on the program as it now is when you close the sheet,
unless it would land on the wrong dance in the same way: then it isn't saved,
and the same message appears.

If you have set calling history to count
only slots marked performed, this is where you mark them — see
[Programs & matrix](./programs.md#track-what-you-have-called).

This sheet belongs to program Perform. Performing a single dance has nothing to
reorder, so it has no adjust sheet.

### Swap a dance

When the floor isn't ready for the dance you planned, swap it. **Swap this
dance** in the adjust sheet swaps the dance you are calling, and the swap
button on each upcoming dance's row swaps that one. Swap is not offered on a
dance you have marked performed, on a note or break, or on a slot before the
current one, nor on an alternate you are showing whose planned dance is already
marked performed. Swapping an upcoming dance leaves you on the card you are reading.

The swap sheet opens already searched. Its **Keep** choices come from the dance
you are swapping:

- **Same formation**, such as Becket;
- **This level or easier**, the dance's level and every easier one;
- **Same type** (contra, English or square), offered only when the program
  mixes types;
- **Not already in tonight's program**; and
- **Figures to keep**: each of the dance's figures, with the phrase it is in.
  Keep one and **In the same phrase** appears for it, and, when a single
  figure comes right before it, **With what comes before it**, which asks for
  that figure right before it too. A figure written as free text is not
  offered, since nothing can search for it.

A filter you have hidden in
[Settings ▸ Collection filters](./settings.md#collection-filters) is not offered
as a Keep choice: hiding Formation, for example, leaves out Same formation.
The figure names follow the dialect of the swapped dance's own type.

Every Keep choice but the figures starts on; you can change which ones in
[Settings ▸ Program ▸ Perform mode ▸ Swap a dance](./settings.md#perform-mode).
The results change as you turn choices on and off, with no Search button. The
slot's own [alternates](./glossary.md#alt) are listed first, under **Your
alternates**, whatever the Keep choices, and the dance you are swapping is never
listed. **More filters** opens the full search panels with the same choices
already set, so you can change them there or add others. From then on the
panels hold your search: **Not already in tonight's program** still changes
which dances are left out, but never undoes what you changed in the panels.

If you have turned on **Suggest the next dance** and **Use in Perform's Swap
this dance** (both in
[Settings ▸ Experimental ▸ Suggestions](./settings.md#suggestions)), the dances
after **Your alternates** are listed in the **Suggested** order for the place of
the dance you are swapping: compared with the dances before and after it, as if
the dance you are swapping out were not there, with a reason under each, as in
the [program builder](./programs.md#let-the-app-suggest-the-next-dance). Your
alternates stay first. Nothing is left out; the same dances are listed as
without it, only in another order. Until the order is worked out the dances
show in their usual order, and you can tap one at any time. English dances are
not ranked yet: they come after the others and say so, and a dance already in
tonight's program (with that Keep choice off) comes last. **Sort** offers the
usual orders too, and **Suggested** there brings this order back. With either
setting off, the swap sheet lists the dances in its usual order.

If you have turned on
[Event rules](./settings.md#event-rules) and the program uses
[rules](./programs.md#check-your-program-against-your-rules), the swap sheet
also checks each dance it lists against them, unless you have turned off
**Check Perform's Swap this dance** in
[Settings ▸ Experimental ▸ Event rules](./settings.md#event-rules) (it is on
by default). A dance that would break a rule if it took the place of the
dance you are swapping shows a marker with the rule's nickname if it has
one, else its sentence, such as
*Would break: At most 1 dance matching "Petronella" in the whole program*
(a screen reader reads a nicknamed rule's sentence too),
and is listed after the dances that wouldn't, in any order, **Suggested**
included. The check counts the program as the swap leaves it: the dance you
swap out stays only as an alternate, which a rule does not count as planned,
so a petronella can replace the program's one petronella without a marker. A rule about
neighboring dances checks the dances on either side. **Your alternates** are
checked too: a marked one stays under **Your alternates**, after the ones
that aren't marked. Nothing is hidden, and you can still tap a marked dance
to swap it in. The dances show at once, without markers, and the markers
appear as soon as the check is done.

Tap a dance to swap it in. The slot keeps its note, guest caller and planned
times; only the dance changes. The dance you swap in is the one being called,
so it takes the planned slot, which counts as called the way any planned dance
does, and the one you swapped out stays in the program as an unmarked
[alternate](./glossary.md#alt), which does not count (see
[calling history](./programs.md#track-what-you-have-called)):

- Swapping a planned dance, the one you swapped out becomes its first
  alternate, right after it.
- Choosing one of **Your alternates** trades the two dances: the planned slot
  takes the alternate's dance, and that alternate takes the dance you swapped
  out, keeping its place.
- If you were calling one of the slot's alternates (with **Show alternate**),
  the slot you are on becomes the planned dance, moved to the front of its
  group, and the dance that was planned becomes its alternate. The dance you
  swapped out is then the first alternate after it, or, if you chose one of
  **Your alternates**, it takes that alternate's place. You stay on the same
  card. Each slot keeps its own note, guest caller and times. In a group that
  starts with a note or a break rather than a dance, the alternate is swapped
  where it is and stays an alternate.

A swap can be undone like any other change. If another device changed the
slots it touches meanwhile, it is not saved; see
[Adjust on the fly](#adjust-on-the-fly). The swap searches only your
collection, never online.

For an English dance, Same formation is not offered, and its figures can be
kept but without the two phrase choices. The swap sheet says so.

## Screen readers and verbose figures

For screen-reader users, Perform mode always announces each figure in an
expanded, spoken-friendly form — clearer to hear than the terse text shown on the
card. You don't need to turn anything on for that; it is how Perform always
behaves.

The **Always show verbose figure text** setting is a different thing: it puts that
fuller wording on screen in the *dance view*, not in Perform. The
[Accessibility guide](./accessibility.md) covers this and the app's other
accessibility options.

## Leaving Perform

Choose **Exit Perform mode** and the app checks first:

> **Exit Perform?** — Leave Perform mode? Your place and the running clock
> are kept, so you can resume where you left off.

On a computer, **Ctrl-W** (**Cmd-W** on a Mac) and a mouse's **Back** button
ask the same question. **Esc** never leaves Perform: when you are calling a
program it closes an open walkthrough, and otherwise it takes the window out of
full screen.

When the stage theme is on, this question — like the other panels Perform
opens, such as **Tap tempo** and **Adjust program** — appears in the stage theme
too, rather than as a bright panel over the dark stage.

**Keep performing** returns you to the card; **Exit** leaves. When you are
calling a program, your place and the clock are kept for as long as that
program stays open, so you can step out to check something and choose **Perform
this program** again to pick up where you left off. That holds when a tablet is
rotated while you perform, too, even though the Programs screen switches
between its one- and two-pane layouts: if it shows the list when you leave,
open the program again. Closing the program — choosing another one, going
back from it to the list, or archiving it (a two-pane screen also closes an
archived program the list stops showing, including when you leave Programs
with **Show archived** on) — or performing a single dance starts the next
session fresh. For that reason the
exit check on a single dance says so instead of promising a resume: *Performing
a single dance doesn't keep your place, so the next time starts fresh.*

If you would rather leave in one tap, turn off **Confirm before leaving
Perform mode** in [Settings ▸ Program ▸ Perform mode](./settings.md#perform-mode).
**Exit Perform mode**, **Ctrl-W** and **Back** then leave straight away. Leaving
that way keeps exactly what the question describes above: a program's place and
clock while the program stays open, and nothing for a single dance.

## Print or share a single dance

You do not have to be on stage to hand someone a dance. From a dance's detail
view you can export or print a single dance card as a PDF or as shareable text,
and the export follows your active dialect (except the role terms of a dance
whose author asked for larks and robins). See
[Share, print & export](./sharing.md#which-words-a-dance-export-uses).
