# Traditional square sources beyond the held squares

Research for [ADR-007](../adr/007-square-dance-canonical-grammar.md) §4.4 and
§7.4, done on 22 November 2026 at the maintainer's request: "Include figures
that our sources indicate are present in trad squares - our held collection
is not comprehensive." Everything below is a reading of the sources in this
note's own words. No definition or dance text is copied.

**Coverage caveat.** The syllabi are long PDFs. For each, about the first
40,000 characters were read in full and the rest only as a machine summary,
so counts below are lower bounds and a call missing here may still appear in
a part not read in full.

## What the wider sources show

- **Structure.** The New England syllabus squares are mostly written as
  intro, figure, break and ending, with a beat count in brackets on each
  line. The older syllabi (1990–2003) put A1–B2 labels on many Parkes, Page
  and Sannella squares; the later ones mostly do not. Singing calls give no
  counts. Southern figures are patter over 16-beat phrases.
- **Calls beyond the held squares**, with the number of distinct squares
  seen (lower bounds) and any count stated:
  - grand right and left: about 20; 16 counts once around (Newport
    Quadrille #3, Odd Couple Promenade with its allemande).
  - four [Robins] chain / grand chain (all four chain across, ¾, or across
    and back): about 10; 8 across (Hofbrau Square), 16 across and back
    (Buffalo Quadrille). Hume's "All Eight Chain" has the [Larks] travel too.
  - grand square and half grand square: about 8; 32 and 16.
  - box the gnat: about 10; 8 (Cross the Way).
  - sashay, half sashay, rollaway: about 10; "slide three steps" 4,
    "rollaway with a half sashay" 8.
  - arches and ducking: arch, duck, dive, "dip and dive" (16 once around in
    Deer Park Lancers), "pop under an arch": about 10.
  - split (split the ring, split those two, go around one or two): about 10;
    8 (Cross the Way).
  - courtesy turn, power turn, double courtesy turn: about 8; 4.
  - weave the ring: about 4.
  - California twirl: about 3.
  - basket swing (three or four dancers): 3.
  - cheat (or swing): 3.
  - bow / honor: about 6, as an opener.
  - wheel around, slide, move up, cut away, back to back, butterfly whirl,
    swat the flea, back-up star, grapevine: one or two each.
  - shoulder round under its discouraged name (right or left shoulder):
    about 4.
  - allemande X (Hume, Caller's Box): an allemande with the corner, pass the
    partner, swing the next.
  - catch all eight (Caller's Box only): half by one hand, all the way by
    the other, with the corner.
  - mountain (Southern) do-si-do: Southern sources dance it without naming
    it; the Caller's Box names it.
- **Southern visiting-couple figures** are whole main parts, not calls:
  Birdie in the Cage, Duck (Dive) for the Oyster, Take a Peek, Mow the
  Wheat, Four Leaf Clover, Georgia Rang Tang, Figure Eight, Texas Star,
  Chase the Rabbit, Divide the Ring, Lady 'Round the Lady, Shoot the
  Buffalo. They are stored as dances whose lines are calls. Their distinctive
  actions (an arch to duck under, peeking round a couple, the bird hopping
  in) become calls only where §7.4 lists them.
- **Not found in the syllabi, *Northern Junket*, the CDSS Southern material
  or the Ontario pages:** Teacup Chain, Ping Pong Circulate, Acey Deucey,
  Thar, Do Paso, Pass the Sea, Trade the Wave, Dixie Style to a Wave. (ADR
  §7.3 found some of these in the Caller's Box and contra sources.) The
  maintainer asked for the first three anyway (ADR §7.2).
